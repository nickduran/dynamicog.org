A body of work on how people take another's perspective to solve problems, modeled as
self-organization under social constraints, and on interpersonal coordination and
co-regulation during collaborative problem solving (CPS) — an essential skill for STEM
learning. This research investigates how interpersonal interactions influence CPS
processes and outcomes in digital learning environments, treating collaboration as
fundamentally about communication and coordination among people who react to and
influence one another. Supported in part by NSF award 1660894 (Role: PI). See related
work in *Journal of Memory and Language* (2019), *Topics in Cognitive Science* (2016),
and *Cognitive Science* (2024).
