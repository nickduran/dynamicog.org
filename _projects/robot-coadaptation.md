As robots move out of controlled settings and into homes, workplaces, and public spaces, the
central problem is no longer how a robot performs a fixed task but how a person and a robot
adapt to each other over repeated interaction. This work brings the lab's methods for
measuring interpersonal coordination - multimodal behavioral capture, linguistic alignment,
and dynamical systems analysis - to bear on human-robot interaction, treating co-adaptation
as a coupled system rather than as a property of either partner alone.

This is a proposed NSF center effort with Joydeep Biswas, Roberto Martin-Martin, Justin
Hart, and Chen Yu at UT Austin.
