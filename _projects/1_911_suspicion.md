We study how listeners form judgments of suspicion from 911 emergency calls — the
behaviors, emotional expressions, and linguistic patterns that lead a caller to be
perceived as a suspect rather than a victim. This work combines behavioral experiments
with computational analysis of call content, examining how violations of emotional
expectations and moral typecasting shape credibility judgments.

Collaborative work with Jessica Salerno and Saul Kassin. Recent results appear in
*Law and Human Behavior* (2026) and *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* (2025).
