When two people interact, complex patterns of behavior emerge spontaneously — organized
around multiple, simultaneously co-occurring types of movement, with little conscious
awareness, yet forming a stable network that guides how people converge on meaning. Taking
a dynamical-systems approach, this line of work examines how acoustic, linguistic, and
movement components interact over time — a focus on **behavioral coordination**, in which
individuals' cognitive processes and behavioral patterns become a coupled system. We study
how coordination shifts under high-level conditions such as deception, persuasion, and
mutual understanding, using automated motion analysis from video and infrared motion
tracking. Supported in part by NSF award 1103356 (Role: PI).
